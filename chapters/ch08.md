# Perturbation of inverses and linear systems

A two-channel instrument reports a strong signal and a weak signal. Reconstructing the weak component requires dividing its reading by its gain. If the gain is miscalibrated, the reconstruction may change substantially even when the coefficient matrix barely changes in norm. The practical question is which error a numerical check can certify.

This chapter uses an original dimensionless calibration model. The recordings supply the sequence of matrix norm equivalence, inverse perturbation, and linear-system perturbation.

## Norms and gain uncertainty ([L27 00:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hxi95z6i7j4&t=16s))

Let $A$ be an invertible square matrix of order $n$, $x$ an unknown input and $b$ its observed output, so $Ax=b$. Write $E$ for a calibration error and $f$ for a measurement error. The perturbed equation is $(A+E)(x+h)=b+f$, where $h$ is the change in the reconstructed input. Use the vector infinity norm $\|x\|_\infty=\max_i|x_i|$ and its induced matrix norm $\|A\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j |a_{ij}|$. In the derivations, $\|\cdot\|$ may be any vector norm and its induced matrix norm. In particular, $\|Ax\|\leq\|A\|\|x\|$ and $\|AB\|\leq\|A\|\|B\|$ for compatible matrices.

Any two norms on a fixed finite-dimensional matrix space are equivalent. To see why, write a matrix as $\sum_{ij}a_{ij}F_{ij}$ with $F_{ij}$ the matrix units. For a norm $N$, the triangle inequality gives $N(A)\leq(\sum_{ij}N(F_{ij}))\max_{ij}|a_{ij}|$. This upper bound applied to $A-B$ makes $N$ continuous in the entries by the reverse triangle inequality. On the compact sphere $\max_{ij}|a_{ij}|=1$, the continuous positive function $N$ has a positive minimum. Scaling gives the reverse bound, and applying the comparison twice gives equivalence for any pair. The constants can depend on dimension. For instance $\|A\|_\infty\leq n\max_{ij}|a_{ij}|$, with equality for the all-ones matrix.

Take $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,\delta)$ with $0<\delta<1$, $b=(1,\delta)^T$, and $E=\operatorname{diag}(0,-\eta\delta)$ with $0<\eta<1$. The nominal solution is $x=(1,1)^T$. The matrix perturbation has infinity norm $\eta\delta$, yet the revised weak component is $1/(1-\eta)$. As $\delta$ shrinks, the same relative error in the weak gain has the same effect on its reconstruction. A mixed-unit model should specify how its variables are scaled before interpreting such normwise comparisons. The LAPACK guide tabulates several common choices for these measures. [LAPACK Users’ Guide, How to Measure Errors](https://www.netlib.org/lapack/lug/node75.html)

## When the inverse survives ([L28 03:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uE0eUFgvn5k&t=187s))

Factor the perturbed coefficient matrix as $(A+E)=A(I_n+B)$, with $B=A^{-1}E$ and $I_n$ the identity. The quantity $q=\|B\|$ measures the gain error after transforming it back to the input coordinates. If $q<1$, the geometric series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-B)^k$ converges in the induced norm because its terms are bounded in norm by $q^k$. Multiplication of the partial sum with $I_n+B$ cancels every interior term and leaves a remainder tending to zero. Thus $I_n+B$ is invertible and its inverse has norm at most $1/(1-q)$.

Subtracting the inverses gives the resolvent identity and a relative bound.

$$
(A+E)^{-1}-A^{-1}=-(I_n+B)^{-1}BA^{-1},
\qquad
\frac{\|(A+E)^{-1}-A^{-1}\|}{\|A^{-1}\|}
\leq \frac{q}{1-q}.
$$

The change begins with $-A^{-1}EA^{-1}$. Indeed $(I_n+B)^{-1}=I_n-B+B^2(I_n+B)^{-1}$, so the discarded inverse correction has norm at most $q^2\|A^{-1}\|/(1-q)$. This also identifies the Fréchet derivative, the linear approximation in $E$ whose remainder is quadratic for fixed $A$.

If computing $q$ is impractical, use $q\leq\|A^{-1}\|\|E\|$. Introduce the condition number $\kappa(A)=\|A\|\|A^{-1}\|$ and the relative coefficient uncertainty $\varepsilon_A=\|E\|/\|A\|$. The sufficient test $\kappa(A)\varepsilon_A<1$ then uses normwise information alone. In the calibration example, $\kappa_\infty(A)=1/\delta$ and $q=\eta$, so both tests agree. The inverse bound is attained because the weak diagonal entry controls $\|A^{-1}\|$ and its change. As $\eta$ tends to one, that entry diverges and the limiting coefficient matrix is singular. In contrast, $E=2A$ gives $q=2$ and $A+E=3A$ remains invertible. The norm condition provides a sufficient guarantee.

## Forward error and backward error ([L29 00:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oe-TgY48Goc&t=17s))

Expanding the perturbed system gives $(A+E)h=f-Ex$. Suppose $x\ne0$ and $q<1$. Apply $(I_n+B)^{-1}A^{-1}$ to this identity and bound the norm.

$$
\frac{\|h\|}{\|x\|}
\leq
\frac{q+\|A^{-1}f\|/\|x\|}{1-q}
\leq
\frac{q+\kappa(A)\|f\|/\|b\|}{1-q}.
$$

The second inequality uses $\|b\|\leq\|A\|\|x\|$. The two terms distinguish model uncertainty and observation uncertainty. If $E=0$ and $f=(0,\delta\alpha)^T$ for scalar $\alpha$, then $h=(0,\alpha)^T$. With $\|x\|_\infty=\|b\|_\infty=1$, this perturbation attains the right-hand-side amplification $\kappa_\infty(A)$.

A computed candidate $\widehat x$ for the nominal problem defines the residual $r=b-A\widehat x$. Its forward error satisfies $x-\widehat x=A^{-1}r$ exactly. To find how credible the candidate is as a solution of nearby data, define the normwise backward error as the smallest $s\geq0$ for which $(A+E)\widehat x=b+f$ with $\|E\|_\infty\leq s\|A\|_\infty$ and $\|f\|_\infty\leq s\|b\|_\infty$. If the denominator below is positive, the answer is

$$
s=\frac{\|r\|_\infty}
        {\|A\|_\infty\|\widehat x\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty}.
$$

The identity $r=E\widehat x-f$ gives the lower bound by the triangle inequality. To attain it when $\widehat x\ne0$, pick a row functional $\phi$ of one-norm one such that $\phi\widehat x=\|\widehat x\|_\infty$. A coordinate of maximal modulus, multiplied by the conjugate of its unit phase, works. Write $d=\|A\|_\infty\|\widehat x\|_\infty+\|b\|_\infty$, then take $E=(\|A\|_\infty/d)r\phi$ and $f=-(\|b\|_\infty/d)r$. Their contributions sum to $r$ and both meet their prescribed budgets. If $\widehat x=0$ and $b\ne0$, use $E=0$ and $f=-b$ to attain $s=1$.

For $\widehat x=(1,0)^T$ in the calibration model, the infinity-norm residual is $\delta$, the backward error is $\delta/2$, and the relative forward error is one. The candidate fits an almost indistinguishable set of output data, but it loses the entire weak component. Conditioning describes the sensitivity of the problem. Whether a solver produces a small backward error depends on its arithmetic. The residual itself is computed in finite precision, so very small residuals may require higher precision to measure credibly.

## Exercises and solutions

1. For the same calibration matrix, place the coefficient perturbation in the strong channel: $E=\operatorname{diag}(-\eta\delta,0)$. Compute $q$ and compare with the normwise test.<br>
   **Solution.** Multiplying by $A^{-1}$ leaves the nonzero entry $-\eta\delta$, so $q=\eta\delta$. The scalar test uses $\kappa_\infty(A)\varepsilon_A=\eta$. The two placements have identical $\|E\|_\infty$. The exact transformed error distinguishes the directions and gives the sharper bound $\eta\delta/(1-\eta\delta)$.

2. Give the smallest $\|E\|_\infty$ for which the calibration matrix can become singular.<br>
   **Solution.** If $\|E\|_\infty<\delta$, then $\|A^{-1}E\|_\infty<1$ and invertibility is guaranteed. At norm $\delta$, the perturbation $\operatorname{diag}(0,-\delta)$ cancels the weak gain. These two facts give the minimum.

3. For the unperturbed coefficient matrix, choose a measurement perturbation with infinity norm $\delta/10$ that gives the smallest possible input change in infinity norm.<br>
   **Solution.** Every $f$ satisfies $\|f\|_\infty=\|Ah\|_\infty\leq\|h\|_\infty$. Select $f=(\delta/10,0)^T$, which gives $h=f$ and attains this lower bound. A perturbation of the same magnitude in the weak channel gives ten percent change.

4. Suppose only $b$ may change. Work out the minimum relative backward error of $(1,0)^T$ when $b=(1,\delta)^T$.<br>
   **Solution.** Fixing $E=0$ forces $f=-r=(0,-\delta)^T$. Dividing its norm by $\|b\|_\infty=1$ gives $\delta$. Allowing both coefficient and right-hand-side changes admits the shared budget $\delta/2$ derived above.

The appropriate diagnostic depends on the certification question. An error bound for the reconstructed input needs conditioning information. A backward error quantifies how much the data need to move to explain a candidate.
