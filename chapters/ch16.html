Chapter 16 · Lectures 61–64

Courant–Fischer and eigenvalue bounds under updates

L61 L62 L63 L64

An engineer can add one spring to an actuator model. The release test now asks whether at least two independent modal stiffnesses reach a threshold. An estimate of the strongest mode gives no answer about the second one. The engineer needs an index-by-index guarantee and a way to identify which sizes of improvement are possible before recomputing all modes.

Use normalized masses and an original model in modal coordinates. Its initial stiffness matrix is \(A=\operatorname{diag}(8,4,2)\). A spring with extension \(z^*x\) for state \(x\) adds the stiffness \(B=zz^*\), where \(z=(0,1,1)^T\). The updated matrix is real symmetric and the target is a second stiffness of at least \(5\). The positive sign of the update models an added passive spring.

Courant–Fischer from intersecting subspaces

L61 01:09 · L62 05:32

For any \(n\)-by-\(n\) Hermitian matrix \(H\) write \(\lambda_1(H)\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n(H)\) for its eigenvalues, in descending order. Let \(q_i\) be the associated orthonormal eigenvectors and \(R_H(x)=x^*Hx/(x^*x)\) for \(x\ne0\). Courant–Fischer gives both formulas

\[\lambda_k(H)= \max_{\dim S=k}\ \min_{0\ne x\in S}R_H(x) =\min_{\dim T=n-k+1}\ \max_{0\ne x\in T}R_H(x).\]

Each inner optimization attains its endpoint because the quotient is continuous on the unit sphere of a nonzero finite-dimensional subspace. Attaining outer subspaces can be exhibited directly.

For the first formula choose \(S_0=\operatorname{span}(q_1,\ldots,q_k)\). Its worst direction gives \(\lambda_k\) by the convex combination formula from Rayleigh–Ritz. An arbitrary \(k\)-space intersects the tail \(\operatorname{span}(q_k,\ldots,q_n)\) because the two dimensions sum to \(n+1\). A nonzero vector in that intersection has quotient at most \(\lambda_k\), so the arbitrary space cannot improve the minimum. For the second formula take \(T_0=\operatorname{span}(q_k,\ldots,q_n)\) with maximum \(\lambda_k\). Any competing \(T\) intersects \(\operatorname{span}(q_1,\ldots,q_k)\), giving a quotient at least \(\lambda_k\). Thus both outer extrema are attained, without requiring a unique optimizing subspace or a spectral gap. The Rayleigh–Ritz endpoints are the cases \(k=1\) or \(k=n\).

The first formula offers a certificate for the actuator target. Exhibit a plane on which every quotient of \(A+B\) is at least \(5\). Choose \(S=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)\), where \(e_i\) is coordinate vector \(i\). For a vector \((a,b,0)^T\) the energy numerator is \(8|a|^2+5|b|^2\). Consequently \(\lambda_2(A+B)\ge 5\) without finding an eigenvector of the updated model. Choosing \(T=\operatorname{span}(e_2,e_3)\) instead gives an upper certificate if one can bound all quotients on \(T\).

Weyl bounds preserve the index

L63 02:40

Let \(A,B\) be Hermitian of the same order. The quotient of \(A+B\) equals the sum of their quotients. The quotient bounds for \(B\) give a pointwise lower and upper bound, and applying either Courant–Fischer formula preserves these inequalities:

\[\lambda_k(A)+\lambda_n(B) \le \lambda_k(A+B) \le \lambda_k(A)+\lambda_1(B).\]

A scalar update \(B=bI_n\) attains both bounds and shifts every stiffness by \(b\). In general they are conservative because the energy directions of the two matrices can differ. In the actuator case \(\lambda(B)=(2,0,0)\), so the interval for the second eigenvalue is \([4,6]\). Here the plane certificate narrows the lower endpoint from \(4\) to \(5\).

More refined Weyl inequalities follow by intersecting spectral subspaces from different matrices. For indices \(i,j\) with \(i+j-1\le n\), take the tails of \(A\) and \(B\) starting at indices \(i,j\). Their intersection has dimension at least \(n-i-j+2\). Each quotient of the sum on it is at most \(\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_j(B)\). Choosing a subspace of the intersection with precisely that dimension, the min–max formula yields

\[\lambda_{i+j-1}(A+B)\le\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_j(B).\]

If \(i+j-n\ge1\), the heads of dimensions \(i,j\) intersect in a space of dimension at least \(i+j-n\). Every quotient of the sum there is at least \(\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_j(B)\). The max–min formula yields

\[\lambda_{i+j-n}(A+B)\ge\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_j(B).\]

The index restrictions ensure that the chosen trial subspace is nonzero. For example, when \(n=4\), tails with \(i=3,j=2\) give an upper bound on \(\lambda_4(A+B)\). Keeping the dimension in the derivation helps prevent such index slips.

In the actuator model the last two coordinates form the block \(\begin{pmatrix}5&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(4\pm\sqrt2\). The updated spectrum is \((8,4+\sqrt2,4-\sqrt2)\), so both the \([4,6]\) bound and the sharper lower certificate are consistent. The weakest mode improved by about \(0.586\). It stayed below the second stiffness even though both eigenvectors rotated within the two-coordinate block.

Positive semidefinite updates and measurement error

L64 00:37 · L64 06:01

A Hermitian \(B\) is positive semidefinite, written \(B\succeq0\), if \(x^*Bx\ge0\) for every \(x\). It is positive definite when the inequality is strict for nonzero \(x\). In spectral coordinates the semidefinite condition holds exactly when \(\lambda_n(B)\ge0\). Therefore each ordered eigenvalue of \(A+B\) for \(B\succeq0\) is at least the corresponding one of \(A\). For positive definite \(B\) every increase is at least \(\lambda_n(B)>0\). Entry signs give neither criterion. The matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\) is positive semidefinite since its form is \(|x_1-x_2|^2\).

The actuator spring has \(x^*Bx=|z^*x|^2\). It leaves all \(x\perp z\) unchanged in quadratic energy, and \(e_1\) remains an eigenvector with stiffness \(8\). For arbitrary nonzero \(z\) and \(\tau>0\), the update \(\tau zz^*\) has rank one with spectrum \((\tau\|z\|_2^2,0,\ldots,0)\). For \(1\le k<n\), the refined upper bound with \(j=2\) gives \(\lambda_{k+1}(A+\tau zz^*)\le \lambda_k(A)\). Together with monotonicity this leaves an alternating spectral pattern. Interlacing develops the corresponding result for adding a coordinate.

For a signed Hermitian measurement error \(E\), write \(\|E\|_2=\max_{x\ne0}\|Ex\|_2/\|x\|_2\) for the induced spectral norm, equal to the maximum eigenvalue modulus by the spectral theorem. The index-preserving bound now gives \(|\lambda_k(H+E)-\lambda_k(H)|\le\|E\|_2\). If \(\|E\|_2\le 0.4\) in the actuator model, the exact updated second stiffness \(4+\sqrt2\) still certifies the \(5\) target for the perturbed model. The plane certificate alone had zero margin. This estimate is a stiffness guarantee under Hermitian perturbations and an explicitly quantified error norm. Eigenvector stability needs additional information about spectral gaps.

Laboratory

Run ch16.py with Python and NumPy. It computes the original, spring-updated and perturbed eigenvalues for deterministic matrices. Assertions check both intersection inequalities for admissible indices and evaluate the plane certificate by computing the smallest eigenvalue of the \(2\)-by-\(2\) restriction. The final check subtracts \(0.4I_3\). This perturbation attains the worst permitted drop for every ordered eigenvalue and leaves the second stiffness about \(5.0142\).

Exercises and solutions

A. Design a robust release certificate

The allowable symmetric measurement error is \(0.45\) in spectral norm. Does the spring model certify the second stiffness target?

Solution. Its margin is \(4+\sqrt2-5=\sqrt2-1\), which is less than \(0.45\). The admissible perturbation \(-0.45I_3\) moves the second eigenvalue below \(5\), so the target can fail.

B. Find a plane with a poor certificate

For the updated actuator model use \(\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_3)\) in the max–min problem. What lower bound does this certify for the second eigenvalue?

Solution. The restriction is diagonal with entries \(8\) and \(3\). The quotient attains \(3\) on \(e_3\), giving the valid lower bound \(3\). The other trial plane in the chapter makes the certificate useful by including the stiffer of the two individual directions in the coupled block.

C. Update the strongest coordinate only

For the original \(A\), add \(2e_1e_1^*\). Compare the Weyl interval for the second stiffness with the actual stiffness.

Solution. The update eigenvalues are \((2,0,0)\), so the Weyl interval is still \([4,6]\). The new matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(10,4,2)\), so the second stiffness stays \(4\). A spring with the same update spectrum can have a different effect through its direction.

D. Show strict growth fails for a rank-one spring

Give a positive semidefinite update to \(\operatorname{diag}(4,4,4)\) whose ordered eigenvalue changes are \((3,0,0)\).

Solution. Use \(3uu^*\) for any unit vector \(u\). It acts as \(3\) along \(u\) and \(0\) on the two-dimensional orthogonal complement. Since the original matrix is \(4I_3\), each direction in that complement retains stiffness \(4\).

E. Read a Weyl index from subspaces

For two Hermitian matrices of order \(6\), use tails starting at \(i=2\) and \(j=3\). Which eigenvalue of their sum has an upper bound from their intersection?

Solution. The tails have dimensions \(5\) and \(4\). Their intersection has dimension at least \(5+4-6=3\). A three-dimensional test subspace in the min–max formula addresses index \(6-3+1=4\). Hence \(\lambda_4(A+B)\le\lambda_2(A)+\lambda_3(B)\).
